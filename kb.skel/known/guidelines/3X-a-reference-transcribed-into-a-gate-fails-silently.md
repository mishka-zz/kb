A reference a gate is scored against is the one piece of arithmetic in the run
that nothing else checks, and writing one down offers ways to get it wrong that
raise nothing.

**A textbook coefficient carries a rounded constant.** A published form is often
written with a constant that folds a physical one in at a few figures. The exact
coefficient is the one to transcribe, and a second arithmetic over the same case
prefers it by a factor in the residual.

**The obvious spelling returns a confident answer at an edge.** A function that
saturates to exactly its limit, feeding one that is infinite there, gives a
finite and wrong quotient with no error - a zero or an infinity standing in the
reference. Writing a quantity as one minus its complement fails the same way at
whichever end is not being looked at.

**There is more than one form in circulation, and they are not near misses.**
Two spellings of what reads as the same quantity can differ by a factor of two
over part of the range, and nothing about either picks between them.

**What checks it is a second arithmetic**, not a second reading: two methods that
share none of their steps, run over the same case, agree or do not, and that
agreement is evidence. Reading the formula back against the book it came from
reproduces whichever transcription was made. And a range the form cannot answer
is refused by the reference rather than answered.
