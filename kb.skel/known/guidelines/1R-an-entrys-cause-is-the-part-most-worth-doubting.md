The entry named a mechanism, and it was right about both specimens it had. Then
a third arrived from outside the corpus with none of that mechanism anywhere in
it and the same symptom, and what produced the symptom was something else
entirely.

The observations were sound and the generalisation over them was not, which is
the ordinary shape of this. A cause is written from the cases in hand, and the
cases in hand are the ones somebody happened to collect - so it is the part of
an entry that has been tested least and reads most confidently.

Doubt the cause first when a new case does not fit, before doubting the
observation. And a fault found by **real traffic** is worth more than one found
by a review, because a review looks where the entries already point - [[1F]] is
the same lesson from the coverage side.

## The address is written from the cases in hand too

An entry that gathers sites and names a file for each is making the same
generalisation one step further out. A cause is a generalisation over the cases
in hand; an address is a note taken while looking at them, and neither is
re-derived when the entry is picked up. It matters more than a wrong cause,
because it fails silently in the reassuring direction: a reader sent to the
named file finds nothing, and *nothing found* reads as *nothing to fix* rather
than as *wrong file*. So a site that comes back clean is a claim to re-open, not
a site to strike off - brief the reader on what to look for and let it find
where.

## And a sentence generalising a measurement is copied into code

A measurement with a table under it can have every row right and both
sentences written over the table wrong: *a surface bounding one volume is on
the outside* was true of every specimen measured and false of the first one
with a void, and it became the wording of a refusal, so the refusal asserted the
opposite of the input. The provenance says `measured:` and the measurement is
not what was wrong. So the mark a reader takes as strength is on the half that
was never in doubt, and the half that generalises it carries no mark at all -
nothing re-derives a sentence.

The tell is not the subject and not the author. It is a sentence that begins
*the same holds*, standing under a provenance earned by something else. A
generalisation is attacked over a population, not re-read - which is the
adversarial tester's step in [[2B]], and is why a new fact earns one.
