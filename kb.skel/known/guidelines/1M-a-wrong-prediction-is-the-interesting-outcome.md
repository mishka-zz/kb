title: A wrong prediction is the interesting outcome, and it is written next to what happened rather than replaced by it
kind: fact
noted: 2026-08-28
tags: method, qa, testing, carried
provenance: judged: the convention a run of sweep transcripts was written under, and the same rule practised outside a sweep - an entry keeping what its own first draft claimed beside what measurement then said
related: 1K, 1N, 1R
---

Where something states what it expects - a step in a plan, an entry's stated
cause, a criterion - and the run says otherwise, both stay in the record.
Overwriting the expectation with the observation loses the only thing the pair
was worth: that somebody's model of the system was wrong here, and by how much
and in which direction.

A step that merely records what happened tests nothing - it cannot fail. A step
that states its expectation can, and the disagreement is the finding even when
the observation turns out to be the correct answer and the expectation the
defect. That case is common enough to be worth expecting.
