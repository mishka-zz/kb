Closing is `SKILL.md` beside `kb.py` under *Closing it*, and it keeps its job: the outcome,
the evidence, the second reader, the `mv`. This is what happens afterwards -
once the change is committed and the work handed over, with nothing left to
decide about it.

**Three questions, and each is a rule already held here.**

- **What was predicted, and what happened?** Where they differ, both are kept,
  because the disagreement is the finding and not the embarrassment ([[1M]]).
  The prediction is in the entry: its stated cause, its criterion, the shape the
  fix was expected to take.
- **What would have been cheaper to know first?** Nearly always a measurement
  that was available before a line was written ([[1Y]]). Naming it is what makes
  the next entry's criterion carry its population.
- **What generalises past this task?** If anything does, it is a guideline and
  is written as one. If nothing does, that is the ordinary answer.

**What it produces is entries, not a document.** `note` for what is still
actionable, and at most one entry in `known/guidelines/`. A review written as
prose beside a live queue reads as current long after it stops being so
([[1N]]), and the record already has a place for each of the things this turns
up.

**Nothing is a result and is said rather than left blank.** A run of these that
produces no guideline is evidence about the work, not about the review.

**It does not replace the owner's audit.** That audit is sharper than this and
reaches things a self-review cannot - a session knows what it meant. This is
what runs when the audit is not available, and it is also what gives the audit
something to disagree with.
