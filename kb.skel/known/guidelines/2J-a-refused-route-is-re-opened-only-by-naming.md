id: 2J
title: A refused route is re-opened by naming what changed under its because, never by being read again
kind: decision
noted: 2026-08-28
decided: 2026-08-28
tags: method, record, queue, carried
because: An entry in refused/ already carries the test for its own re-opening - `because` names the reasoning that would have to fail - so the trigger is written into the entry and the format needs nothing added. The opposite habit costs the store its value: a rejected idea read on the way past is read in the same voice as a live one, and a record that cannot keep the two apart is worse than one that never kept the rejection at all.
related: 1V, 1Y, 1B, 1M
---

Reading `refused/` at pickup is right and is the point of keeping it - working
without the roads already put down is how a rejected design gets proposed again
with confidence. `find --near` returns those entries and prints the place beside
each, so a refused entry is never anonymous.

The hazard is not in the reading. It is in acting on what was read while the
place has stopped registering, which happens on the fifth entry of a listing and
not on the first.

**So re-opening is an act with a precondition, and the precondition is external.**
Something moved that the `because` rested on: a corpus grew, a measurement
landed, a release changed what a layer does, an upstream behaviour turned out
otherwise. *It might work now* is not one of these, and neither is a fresh
reading of the same entry.

**The procedure, and it does not touch the old entry.** [[1V]] holds: editing a
terminal entry in place destroys the pair that is the record. So

- write a **new** entry for the route, stating it in the present tense, with
  `related:` naming the refused one;
- say in its body which clause of that `because` is no longer true, and what
  made it false;
- leave the refused entry exactly where it is. It was correct on the day, and
  the record of a belief that was right and then stopped being right is the
  thing worth having.

**An idea taken out of `refused/` with no entry behind it is the failure this
store exists to prevent**, arriving by the one route the store cannot see. The
cost of the rule is one entry; without it the queue silently regrows what it
already declined.

Re-opening a route is also not the same as the route being right - the new entry
is work, and [[1Y]] applies to it like any other: its premise is a hypothesis,
and the cheap measurement comes before the code.
