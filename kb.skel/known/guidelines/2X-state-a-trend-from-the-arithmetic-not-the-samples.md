    was read as monotone, and a pair of inputs written by hand peaks in the

A sweep hands back a list of points. What gets written down is a shape - "it
climbs as the ratio falls", "it settles", "it is worse below this value" - and
that shape is a claim about every point between the ones that were run,
including the ones a sweep of any fineness would still not have shown.

That record's sweep was measured at every step over the whole corpus and
climbed on every specimen that moved at all. What that says is that the corpus
has no interior peak. What was read off it is that the figure is monotone in
the parameter, which is false: two demands that one covers at a shallow setting
and the other swallows at a steep one bind between the two and nowhere else. Two
inputs written by hand show it in a second, and no corpus run would ever have.

**The arithmetic is what settles the shape, and it is usually two lines.**
Write the quantity down as a function of the parameter, ask where its pieces
change which one is binding, and the interior extremum is there or it is not.
The same class of error had landed three days earlier the other way round -
writing down which direction a slope covers from a sentence that sounded right
when one line of algebra settles it.

So: do the algebra on the quantity before sweeping it, and where a sweep is the
evidence, write down the range the claim is made over rather than the direction
it was going. [[2N]] is the neighbouring rule and a different one - it is about
carrying a sweep past where you meant to stop, which finds a bound; this is
about what happens between the points, which no fineness reaches.

A quantity nobody has shown to be monotone is one where the worst case may be
anywhere, and a bound fitted to an end of the range is then a bound on nothing.
A share read off a sweep of a decision threshold is the ordinary case: it is a
claim about every threshold between the ones run, and the curve it samples is
not promised to be smooth.
