A constant this project *chose* is a different thing from one it measured, and a
choice sitting on a measurement has to say which part is which. This is the
failure that survives that separation: the choice can be right and the account
of where it sits can be false.

Constants are held to saying **why**, and bounded at both ends where anything
bounds them. That is right, and it has a failure the rule itself invites: once a
number has been measured, a reason gets written to sit underneath it, and the
reason is not measured by anything.

The entry fitted a ratio to a gap it had swept the whole corpus for - artefacts
on one side, real cases on the other, nothing in between - and then explained
the artefact side with a general argument about what the domain can produce. A
specimen in the corpus the sweep had already run over is a counterexample to
that argument.

**The number survived and the sentence did not**, which is the shape to expect:
a fitted constant is answerable to the measurement, and the story is answerable
to nothing. So the story is the part to attack, and the place to attack it from
is the population already in hand - not a new experiment.

That changes how a constant is written.

**Say what it costs, not what it proves.** The bound that survived is not about
what the domain can produce; it is about what such a case *does* when it is
kept - it costs something downstream, and that cost is checkable against the
code without a theorem about the domain.

**A justification that reaches outside this repository is the same fact as any
other**, so it is measured in the session, cited at a file and line, or not
written. A claim about what some external system does, stated calmly beside a
number that is right, reads as settled and is the harder kind to catch - nobody
re-derives the prose under a passing test.

The corollary for review: an adversarial reader given the measurements is worth
most on the *reasoning*, not on the result. Both rounds confirmed the fix and
overturned an argument for it, and the second found a defect in the measure
itself - the quantity the ratio was read from answered a different question than
the one it was named for.
